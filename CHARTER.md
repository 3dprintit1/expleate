# The Expleate charter

Expleate is a place where anyone in the world can pool resources with anyone else, for projects of creativity, adventure and joy. Someone suggests a project, and everyone else decides for themselves whether to put something in, and how much.

## The world we build for

Imagine that everyone already had an equal share of the world's resources. Nobody would need to sell anything to anybody. Nobody would put something in hoping to get more back. People would simply decide, together, what to do with what they have.

Expleate is built as if that world were already here. Every decision about how it works, and how it is run, starts from there. In practice that means:

- Nobody profits. Expleate takes no fee, and nobody earns anything from a pool.
- One person has one voice. Putting in more never buys more say.
- What you pool stays yours to take back, as far as the project has not used it yet.
- Everything done with pooled resources happens in the open.

## What projects are for

Every project on Expleate follows six rules.

### Creativity, adventure or joy

A project is something people can share in: making things, exploring, playing, celebrating. A mural on a railway bridge. A group rowing the length of a river in a boat they built. A street piano. A night of stargazing for whoever turns up. An opera written by a whole village.

### No politics

Projects have nothing to do with politics. No parties, elections, campaigns, lobbying, protests or government, whichever side they are on.

### No war

Projects have nothing to do with war or the military. That includes help for people caught up in a war, however good the cause. It is the hardest line in this charter, and it is drawn on purpose.

### No financial gain

Nobody profits from a pool. So:

- nothing is sold, and nobody invests
- no wages, fees or salaries are paid from a pool, to hosts or to anyone else
- there are no prizes of money
- there are no businesses, start-ups or products

Pools pay for what a project itself needs: materials, tools, travel, a venue. In a world of equal shares nobody needs paying to take part. People join in because they want to.

### Not charity

Expleate is not for charity or fundraising. Pools never go to relief, aid or appeals. Those causes matter and have their own homes. Expleate starts from a world where nobody needs rescuing, and pools resources for the joy of doing things together.

### Do no harm

Projects must not hurt people, animals or places, and must be lawful where they happen.

## How pooling works

1. Anyone, or any group, can suggest a project.
2. Anyone can put in any amount they like.
3. You can take back your portion whenever you like, all of it or some of it.
4. You can never take back more than you put in.
5. When the project uses resources from the pool, everyone in the pool carries that use in proportion to their part of the pool. If nothing has been used since you joined, you can take back everything you put in. If some has been used, you can take back your fair share of what is left.
6. When a project finishes, whatever is left goes back to the people in the pool, in the same fair shares.

People joining or leaving a pool never changes what your portion is worth. Only the project using resources does that, and it affects everyone by the same proportion. [How pooling works](docs/pooling.md) explains it with examples.

## Hosting a project

Whoever suggests a project hosts it. When a group suggests one, everyone in the group is a host, and groups have no owners or admins.

- Hosts record every use of the pool and what it was for. Anyone can read these.
- Hosts share news, so everyone in the pool can see where the project is heading and decide whether to stay in.
- Pooled resources go to the project itself, never to anyone as pay.
- Hosts can finish a project at any time, and whatever is left goes back to the people in the pool.

## When a project might break the charter

When someone suggests a project, an automatic check looks for words that often mean a project is about politics, war, financial gain or charity. The check never turns anything down by itself. If it finds something, the proposer can reword the project, or explain why it still fits.

Anyone can flag a project they think breaks the charter. Once three different people have flagged the same project, its pool pauses.

Either way, a **charter circle** is drawn: a handful of members picked at random, a bit like a jury. Nobody who hosts the project, has pooled into it or has flagged it can be drawn. Each person in the circle has one vote, and votes are anonymous.

A majority of the circle decides. If the circle runs out of time first, the project is only stopped when more of the circle said it breaks the charter than said it fits. If a circle finds that a project breaks the charter, its pool closes and everything left goes back to the people in it.

Taking your portion back is never paused, whatever a circle is doing.

## Running costs

Keeping Expleate online costs money, for hosting and a domain name. Every cost is published on the [running costs page](/costs) with its receipt.

Gifts cover costs first, and the founder covers the first $200. After that, what is owed is shared across every live pool at exactly what it cost, once a month. Each pool gives up the same small percentage of what it holds, and a single share never takes more than 2% of everything pooled. Nobody takes a margin, a fee or a salary.

Caretakers are the people who pay the bills and record them. They have no say over projects or circles.

## Fairness and privacy

- Nobody can see how much anyone else has pooled. A pool shows how many people are in it and what it holds.
- There are no leaderboards and no rankings by money. Projects are listed in an order that is reshuffled every day, so every project takes its turn near the top.
- You choose whether your name appears among the people in a pool. Amounts never do.
- A pool's record lists every movement in and out, so anyone can check the books. Only uses are tied to a name: the host who recorded them.

## Changing this charter

The charter belongs to everyone who uses Expleate. Changes are proposed in the open, where anyone can read and comment on them. Giving members a way to agree changes together, on Expleate itself, is next on the list.
